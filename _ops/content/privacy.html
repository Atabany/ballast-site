This version describes Ballast 1.1 and later. Earlier versions call the Blocker "Shield". Versions before 1.1 have no Blocker or other Family Controls features, and the rest of this policy applies to them too.

What Ballast stores on your device

All of it stays in Ballast's own storage on your iPhone, and Ballast doesn't transmit any of it:

  • the answers you give during setup: how often and for how long, your hardest time of day, what you've tried, what you want back and the triggers you pick, and the goal you choose
  • your start date, streaks, days free, the highest level you've reached, and logged urges and slips, including any triggers, notes and the tools you used during an urge
  • your daily check-ins and written reflections
  • your reasons, and the optional picture you can keep with them
  • your urge plans and any plan drafts
  • your course progress and the answers you write in it, and your weekly reviews
  • your habits and their completion history
  • which articles you've read, milestones you've passed, and what you've ticked in the nine steps and in Cut the easy access
  • your reminder settings
  • the name, phone number and message for your optional support contact
  • your Blocker settings, including any cool-off you chose and a salted hash of a trusted-person code (not the code itself), and the websites you add to the filter

Deleting the app deletes it from the phone. Ballast keeps no other copy, and we have no way to recover it or reach it.

Apple's device backups are the exception Ballast doesn't control. If iCloud Backup is on, or you back the phone up to a Mac or PC, those backups can include Ballast's data automatically, under Apple's terms, and can outlast deleting the app. You can turn this off for Ballast in Settings, under your Apple Account, iCloud, Manage Account Storage, Backups.

The Blocker and Apple's Family Controls

The Blocker uses Apple's Family Controls and Managed Settings, with your permission, to turn on the adult-website filter and to block apps you choose. Here's what that means for your data:

  • No browsing data reaches Ballast. Ballast tells iOS to turn the filter on. iOS does the filtering. Ballast never sees which sites you visit or which were blocked.
  • Sites you add. The websites you add to the filter stay on your phone and are handed to iOS, which does the blocking. Ballast doesn't send them anywhere, and they're never in a backup file, an export, a widget, a notification or a log. Apple's device backups can include them, as described above.
  • No app data reaches Ballast. You choose apps in Apple's own picker, and Ballast receives opaque tokens to pass back to iOS. It can't read the names of the apps you picked and doesn't see how you use them. Your choices stay on your device.
  • Nothing about this leaves your phone from Ballast. No browsing or app-usage data is read by Ballast or sent to us or anyone else by Ballast, because there's no server to send it to. Apple's own handling of iOS features is covered by Apple's privacy policy.
  • Trusted-person code. If you use the commitment lock, Ballast makes a code and you send it yourself, once, through the iOS share sheet to whoever you choose, using whichever app you choose. That message is between you, them and that app. Ballast keeps only a salted hash and forgets the code.
  • You can withdraw permission at any time in Settings. If the Blocker was on, its screen will then say "Not filtering": Screen Time permission is off, so iOS isn't filtering.

What leaves your device

Purchases. Ballast uses RevenueCat to manage subscriptions and restore purchases. Every time you open Ballast or come back to it, and when you buy, restore or open the paywall, Ballast asks RevenueCat whether you have Pro. Each time, RevenueCat receives purchase and receipt information, an anonymous app user ID it generates, a device identifier, and basic app and OS version details. This is used to work out whether you have an active subscription, and nothing else. It is not used for advertising, and it is not shared with data brokers. RevenueCat's own policy is at revenuecat.com/privacy.

Apple. Purchases are processed by Apple under Apple's own privacy policy. We never see your payment details.

That is the complete list. There is no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, no crash reporter, and no third-party tracking of any kind in this app.

Files you choose to share. Anything you send through the share sheet goes only where you send it: a data export, a backup file, an appointment summary, a picture of your days free and level, or a check-in card for someone you trust. A backup holds your private entries, so keep it somewhere you trust. Restoring reads the file on your phone.

Things people reasonably assume, that aren't true here

  • Ballast does not read your browsing, your screen time, or any other app. It knows only what you tell it and the settings you choose.
  • Ballast does not access your photo library. The optional picture beside your reasons is chosen through Apple's photo picker, which runs outside the app: Ballast is handed the one image you pick and nothing else. The date and location your camera attached to it are removed before it is saved.
  • Ballast does not access your contacts. The optional support contact is a name and number you type yourself, stored on your phone. Ballast never uploads it.
  • Ballast does not send messages for you. The "Text" button opens Apple's Messages app with your message already written. You decide whether to send it.
  • Face ID, Touch ID and passcode. If you turn on the app lock, they are checked entirely by iOS. Ballast is told only whether it succeeded. Your biometric data never reaches the app.
  • Notifications are scheduled locally on your device. Nothing is sent through a server.
  • Widgets, the Panic button in Control Centre and Siri shortcuts read from and write to a shared container on your device only. The timer on your Lock Screen during an urge is a Live Activity that the app starts and updates on your phone, with no push server. Nothing about any of them goes through a server.

Tracking

Ballast does not track you. It does not link your data with third-party data for advertising, and it does not share data with data brokers. It never requests the advertising identifier.

Your data, your control

  • Export. Settings → Export my data produces a JSON file of your start date and basic settings such as the triggers you pick and your reminder times, your urges and slips, check-ins, reasons and habits, to read or send wherever you like. It holds only those, so your other setup answers (how often and for how long, your hardest time of day, what you've tried and what you want back), goal, level, urge plans, course answers, steps, weekly reviews, support contact, the picture and the tools you used during urges aren't in it. The backup below is the full copy.
  • Backup and restore. A backup is the full copy of your history, plans and progress, including your setup answers, goal, level and the picture: a file you save through the share sheet and restore yourself. Ballast keeps no copy. Blocker settings, including the sites you add, stay on this phone and aren't in the file.
  • Delete. Deleting the app removes everything Ballast stored on the device. Files you saved elsewhere, and Apple device backups already made, stay where they are. Apple doesn't document whether the website filter or blocked apps outlast deleting the app, so they may. If something is still blocked afterwards, look in Settings, under Screen Time. For purchase records held by RevenueCat, email us and we will request deletion on your behalf.
