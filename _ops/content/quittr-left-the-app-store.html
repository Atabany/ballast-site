How to check and cancel a subscription through Apple

Deleting an app does not cancel its subscription. These steps are from Apple's cancellation guide:

  1. Open Settings and tap your name.
  2. Tap Subscriptions.
  3. Tap the subscription.
  4. Tap Cancel Subscription. You may need to scroll. If there's no Cancel button, or you see an expiration message in red, it's already cancelled.

Can't find it? Search your email for "receipt from Apple" or "invoice from Apple" to see which Apple Account paid. If you find no Apple receipt, you may have paid another way: check your card statement and QUITTR's support page, which has its own cancellation and refund pages. On Android, use Google Play's subscriptions screen.

Refunds. You can ask Apple. Sign in at reportaproblem.apple.com, choose "I'd like to", then "Request a refund", pick your reason and the subscription, and submit. Apple says to wait 24 to 48 hours for an update. Some purchases are eligible and some aren't, so there's no promise here, but it's reasonable to ask if you paid for time you can't use.

How to keep your start date

The date is the part that's yours, and it's easy to keep. Do this before you delete anything.

  1. If the app still opens, screenshot your streak, your start date and anything you wrote that you want to keep.
  2. Write the date down somewhere that isn't the app: your notes, your calendar, on paper.
  3. Write down what you've learned too. Your triggers and what's helped are worth more than the number, and a plan sheet is a good place for them.

Don't count on any app importing a streak from another. Ballast doesn't. Its setup simply asks how long since the last time, so you can carry your count across by hand; any tracker can do the same. If the number is lost, what you did during those days isn't.

Your account data

  • Look for a delete option in the app's settings before you remove it, if it still opens.
  • If there's no option, send QUITTR a written deletion request through its support page and keep a copy. Depending on where you live, privacy law may give you the right to have it deleted.
  • Change reused passwords. If you used the same password anywhere else, change it there.
  • Be wary of unexpected messages that mention your use of the app, especially ones offering refunds or asking for money. Go directly to Apple or to the company's own site instead. If a message threatens to expose you, don't pay; contact your local police.

If you relied on its blocking

Test it, and don't assume you're still covered. Apple's built-in Screen Time filter is free, takes about five minutes and doesn't depend on any app staying in the store. Set it up on a calm day rather than on the night you need it.

How to choose what's next

You may not need another app. A filter, a written plan and one person you can talk to cover a lot. If you do want one, these questions come from the complaints I read most often in this category's App Store reviews:

  • Do you see what it costs before a long quiz? Being asked to pay after a long setup is the most common complaint.
  • Is help free at the moment you need it?
  • Where does your data live, and does it need an account? Read the privacy label and the policy.
  • Can you get your data out and restore it? Lost streaks are a repeated complaint.
  • What happens to blocking if you stop paying? It should release cleanly.
  • What does it claim? Be wary of countdowns and brain claims. Here's what the research supports.
  • Do you want a community or a faith-based app? Those are fair things to want, and some apps do them well.
