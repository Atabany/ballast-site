What isn't supported

"Dopamine detox" and "rewiring your brain in 90 days." These are the category's favourite claims. I couldn't find trials showing that a fixed number of days does anything specific to the brain of someone quitting porn, and none of the studies above measured such a thing. Be careful with any app that gives you a countdown to being fixed.

Withdrawal as a certainty. In a randomised study by Fernandez and colleagues, 2023 (176 students who regularly watched), a week of abstinence produced no significant difference from controls in craving, mood or withdrawal-type symptoms. It's one short study in a non-clinical sample, so it doesn't settle anything about longer attempts or people in more distress. If you do feel bad when you stop, that's real and worth taking seriously; it just isn't a universal law.

A question worth asking first

Is this causing problems in your life, or does it conflict with your values, or both? Grubbs and colleagues, 2019 argue that some distress about porn comes partly from moral incongruence: behaviour that clashes with what you believe. That model is contested. Whatever you conclude, it's useful to know which of those you're working on, and a good therapist can help you sort it out. Your reasons for changing are yours and don't need anyone's approval.

What to do this week

None of these is risky or expensive, and most of them have some support from the work above, even where it wasn't tested on porn directly.

  1. Keep a short log for a week. When an urge shows up, note the time, where you were and what was going on. Most people's urges cluster around a few situations.
  2. Write one if-then plan for your most common situation. Make it physical and small: "If it's late and I'm in bed with my phone, the phone goes on the kitchen charger." The free plan builder walks you through it.
  3. Add friction. Turn on the free Screen Time filter, log out of the easiest ways in, and move the charger out of the bedroom.
  4. Practise riding out one urge. Set a ten-minute timer. Notice where you feel it, breathe slowly and watch whether it rises and falls. You're practising a different response, not trying to make the urge disappear.
  5. Decide now what happens after a slip. The hours afterwards matter. Here's a plan you can adopt before you need it.
  6. Tell one person, if that feels possible. It's optional, and for many people it changes how heavy this feels.

Questions

What is the most effective way to stop watching porn?

The best-studied option is therapy using CBT or ACT, which showed large effects on use in a 2025 review of 20 studies, with a warning about high risk of bias. No approach works for everyone, and evidence for self-help and apps is much thinner.

Is porn addiction a real diagnosis?

The World Health Organization's ICD-11 lists compulsive sexual behaviour disorder (code 6C72), and only a clinician can say whether it fits. Researchers still disagree about whether "addiction" is the right word. The WHO also notes that distress coming only from moral or religious disapproval is not enough on its own.

How long does it take to stop?

Nobody can honestly give you a number. The studies here don't support a fixed timeline, and people vary a great deal. It's more useful to track what changes in your own days than to count down to a date.

Do I have to quit completely?

That's your decision. Some people want none at all and others want to cut back; research on therapy has measured both reduced use and stopping. If you aren't sure, a therapist can help you think it through without judging your answer.
