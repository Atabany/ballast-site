## Plain product summary

What it is: an iPhone app that helps adults quit porn and compulsive masturbation (PMO). Made by one independent developer, Mohamed Elatabany. Requires iOS 26 or later. iPhone only. The live App Store name is "Ballast: Quit PMO", App Store ID 6803731313.

Three parts:

- Shield. One tap turns on the iOS adult-website filter through Apple's Family Controls (free). It also disables Safari private browsing. Apple's filter can miss sites, so people can add their own: 3 free, up to 25 with Pro, kept on the phone and handed to iOS. Pro adds a commitment lock (a cool-off chosen in advance of 10 minutes, 1 hour or 24 hours, or a code held by a trusted person, with a 24-hour wait always available) and app shields until a chosen time. A phone's owner can undo all of this in Settings. If Pro ends, locks release and the filter stays under the user's control.
- Urge plans. One tap on what's happening shows the user's own if-then plan and own reason, then a choice of breathing, riding the wave or doing the planned action, then an outcome. The whole flow is free. One plan is free; unlimited plans are Pro.
- Voyage. Clear days accumulate and never reset. The current run is shown second. Waypoints at 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60, 100, 180 and 365 clear days. Slips appear as ordinary marks. Insights, full history and a weekly review are Pro.

Free: whole urge flow, slip logging and after-slip support; streak, clear days, route, check-ins, today's watch; one-tap adult-website filter and 3 sites of your own; one urge plan and one habit; course week one and all reading about urges and slips; toolbox, friction checklist, getting help, partner card; app lock, export, backup and restore.

Pro: commitment lock; app shields; up to 25 sites of your own; unlimited urge plans; insights and weekly review; course weeks 2 to 4 and the rest of the library; full history and calendar; unlimited habits. Sold as monthly, yearly or lifetime. Prices are shown in the App Store.

Privacy: no account and no server. Personal entries stay on the device, though Apple's iCloud or computer backups of the phone can include app data. Ballast asks RevenueCat whether the user has Pro at each launch and foreground. No browsing or app data leaves the phone; Apple's picker hides app choices from Ballast. Apple and RevenueCat process purchases. No analytics or advertising SDKs.

Limits: it is not treatment and does not diagnose. It has no community or groups. It cannot stop a determined device owner. There is no outcome data for the app and no App Store ratings yet. It makes no claims about dopamine, rewiring or fixed timelines; it uses skills from CBT and ACT, and research on those is promising with a high risk of bias.

Who it may not suit: people who want a community, a faith-based program, reports sent to another person, or Android.
