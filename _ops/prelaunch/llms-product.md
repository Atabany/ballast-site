## Plain product summary

What it is: an iPhone app that helps adults quit porn and compulsive masturbation (PMO). Made by one independent developer, Mohamed Elatabany. Requires iOS 26 or later. iPhone only. The live App Store name is "Ballast: Quit PMO", App Store ID 6803731313, version 1.0.1.

What is in the app today:

- Urge flow. Four steps: guided breathing, the user's own reasons, a timed urge-surfing exercise with their own if-then plan, an idea for something to do, grounding exercises and a person to text, then a short check-in. It opens from the app, a Control Centre button or a Siri shortcut, and shows a Lock Screen countdown. The whole flow is free.
- Slips and streak. Time since the quit date, urge and slip logging, milestones, Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets. A slip restarts the current run; the longest run and history stay. After a slip the app offers a few small things for the next hour, and a quiet card the day after.
- Daily work. Check-ins and a journal, a guided 30-day course, short reading, if-then plans, habits, a grounding toolbox, nine one-off steps and a friction checklist of seven changes to the phone.
- Insights (Pro). Trigger summaries and a day-and-hour heatmap from the user's own entries. Patterns, not predictions.

Website filter: Ballast 1.0.1 doesn't include a filter. Its friction checklist walks through Apple's free Screen Time steps.

Coming in the next update: a built-in adult-website filter and progress a slip can't erase.

Free: whole urge flow, slip logging and after-slip support; streak and daily check-ins; grounding toolbox and friction checklist; one replacement habit and one if-then plan; seven days of journal and the current calendar month; course week one and all reading about urges and slips; app lock and data export.

Pro: Insights; the remaining course days and the rest of the reading library; full journal and calendar history; extra habits and plans; the weekly review. Sold as monthly, yearly or lifetime. Prices are shown in the App Store.

Privacy: no account and no server. Personal entries stay on the device, though Apple's iCloud or computer backups of the phone can include app data. Ballast asks RevenueCat whether the user has Pro. Ballast does not read browsing or other apps. Apple and RevenueCat process purchases. No analytics or advertising SDKs.

Limits: it is not treatment and does not diagnose. It has no community or groups. It has no website filter yet. There is no outcome data for the app and no App Store ratings yet. It makes no claims about dopamine, rewiring or fixed timelines; it uses skills from CBT and ACT, and research on those is promising with a high risk of bias.

Who it may not suit: people who want a community, a faith-based program, reports sent to another person, blocking built into the app, or Android.
