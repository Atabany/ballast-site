Privacy Policy

Updated 4 November 2026

This version describes Ballast 1.1 and later, which adds Shield. Earlier versions have no Family Controls features, and the rest of this policy applies to them too.

What Ballast stores on your device

All of it stays in Ballast's own storage on your iPhone, and none of it is transmitted anywhere:

  • your start date, streaks, clear days, and logged urges and slips, including any triggers and notes
  • your daily check-ins and written reflections
  • the reasons you write during setup, and the optional picture you can keep with them
  • your urge plans, your habits and their completion history
  • which articles you've read, milestones you've celebrated, and program steps you've completed
  • the name, phone number and message for your optional support contact
  • your Shield settings, including any cool-off you chose and a salted hash of a trusted-person code (not the code itself)

Deleting the app deletes all of it. There is no copy anywhere else unless you made one yourself, and no way for me to recover it. That's the trade for there being no way for anyone else to reach it either.

Shield and Apple's Family Controls

Shield uses Apple's Family Controls and Managed Settings, with your permission, to turn on the adult-website filter and to shield apps you choose. Here's what that means for your data:

  • No browsing data reaches Ballast. Ballast tells iOS to turn the filter on. iOS does the filtering. Ballast never sees which sites you visit or which were blocked.
  • No app data reaches Ballast. You choose apps in Apple's own picker, which lets you specify apps without revealing your choices to the app. Ballast receives opaque tokens to pass back to iOS. It can't read the names of the apps you picked and doesn't see how you use them.
  • Nothing about this leaves your phone from Ballast. No browsing or app data is sent to me or to anyone else by Ballast, because there's no server to send it to. Apple's own handling of iOS features is covered by Apple's privacy policy.
  • Trusted-person code. If you use the commitment lock, Ballast generates a code and you send it yourself, once, through the iOS share sheet to whoever you choose, using whichever app you choose. That message is between you, them and that app. Ballast keeps only a salted hash and forgets the code.
  • You can withdraw permission at any time in Settings. The Shield screen will then say the shield is down.

What leaves your device

Purchases. Ballast uses RevenueCat to manage subscriptions and restore purchases. When you buy, restore, or open the paywall, RevenueCat receives purchase and receipt information, an anonymous app user ID it generates, a device identifier, and basic app and OS version details. This is used to work out whether you have an active subscription, and nothing else. It is not used for advertising, and it is not shared with data brokers. RevenueCat's own policy is at revenuecat.com/privacy.

Apple. Purchases are processed by Apple under Apple's own privacy policy. I never see your payment details.

That is the complete list. There is no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, no crash reporter, and no third-party tracking of any kind in this app.

Files you choose to share. A data export, backup file or appointment summary goes only where you send it through the share sheet. A backup holds your private entries, so keep it somewhere you trust. Restoring reads the file on your phone.

Things people reasonably assume, that aren't true here

  • Ballast does not read your browsing, your screen time, or any other app. It knows only what you type into it and the settings you choose.
  • Ballast does not access your photo library. The optional picture beside your reasons is chosen through Apple's photo picker, which runs outside the app: Ballast is handed the one image you pick and nothing else. The date and location your camera attached to it are removed before it is saved.
  • Ballast does not access your contacts. The optional support contact is a name and number you type yourself, stored on your device. Ballast never uploads it.
  • Ballast does not send messages for you. Tapping "Text" beside a contact opens Apple's Messages app with a draft. You decide whether to send it.
  • Face ID / Touch ID. If you turn on the app lock, authentication is handled entirely by iOS. Ballast is told only whether it succeeded. Your biometric data never reaches the app.
  • Notifications are scheduled locally on your device. Nothing is sent through a server.
  • Widgets and Siri shortcuts read from a shared container on your device only.

Tracking

Ballast does not track you. It does not link your data with third-party data for advertising, and it does not share data with data brokers. It never requests the advertising identifier.

Your data, your control

  • Export. Settings → Export my data produces a complete JSON file of everything Ballast holds, which you can save or send wherever you like.
  • Backup and restore. A backup is a file you save through the share sheet and restore yourself. Ballast keeps no copy.
  • Delete. Deleting the app removes everything on the device. For purchase records held by RevenueCat, email me and I will request deletion on your behalf.

This website

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from third parties. The plan builder runs in your browser and sends nothing anywhere. The host, GitHub Pages, may keep ordinary server logs under GitHub's own policy.

Children

Ballast is rated 16+ and is not directed at children under 13. I do not knowingly collect any information from children under 13.

Not medical advice

Ballast is a self-tracking and educational tool. It is not treatment, it does not diagnose anything, and it is not a substitute for professional care. If this is affecting your work, your relationships or your safety, please talk to someone qualified.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and material changes will be noted in the app's release notes.
